Supporting dissertation page 62

This page provides model tables for the additional Q1 robustness checks discussed in dissertation Section 5.4.9 (printed pages 61-62). These checks examine whether estimated price changes associated with FEMA map updates depend on geographic proximity or on controls for local housing-market conditions.

Reading the model tables

The outcome is the logarithm of sale price. The models include property (pin) fixed effects and cluster standard errors at the 10-digit parcel (pin10) level. The geographic time controls vary by section. Preliminary and effective map designations are estimated separately; within each section, the preliminary-map table precedes the effective-map table.

In the tables labeled I and II, I estimates the added-to-SFHA indicator and II estimates the removed-from-SFHA indicator. The corresponding Subset columns exclude the opposite transition and the always-SFHA group (for additions) or never-SFHA group (for removals). Where shown, Symmetric uses the in-SFHA indicator, while column 0 includes addition and removal indicators together. Positive coefficients indicate higher estimated sale prices and negative coefficients indicate lower estimated prices; a log coefficient b converts to a percentage change as 100 * (exp(b) - 1). Stars and standard errors should be read alongside the sample and fixed-effects specification.

These tables use the existing prepared dataset outputs/q2_marginaleffects/bldg_sfha_q2_dissertation_prepped.RDS; despite its folder name, the models below use Q1 FEMA map-transition indicators. The 300-meter indicator comes from data/processed/bldgswithin300mof_SFHA_arcgis.xlsx, matched on PIN10. This page documents the implemented samples. It does not establish that the current tables reproduce the exact 15.9% and 18.1% estimates reported in the dissertation; that requires a separate check of the original data vintage, model, and coefficient transformation.

Nearby control properties only

The first robustness check asks whether comparisons among geographically close properties produce similar results to the broader Q1 analysis. All observations in these models are restricted to nearby_300 == TRUE, not just control observations. Membership is defined by the existing GIS proximity list; the buffer geometry is not reconstructed here. The first table shows separate preliminary/effective addition and removal models with property and year fixed effects. The following tables also show the restricted comparison groups described above.

Year-by-municipality fixed effects

This implements the municipality-year check described on dissertation page 62. The models use property fixed effects plus sale_year^clean_name, allowing each municipality to have its own effect in each year. They retain the 300-meter sample restriction. Differences from the property-plus-year models therefore reflect the additional geographic time controls within that restricted sample.

That distinction matters when comparing these results with the dissertation’s description.

Compare like-for-like transition indicators and map timing across the nearby-sample and spillover-exclusion tables; sample composition differs between the checks.

## Reading the model tables

The outcome is the logarithm of sale price. The models include property (`pin`) fixed effects and cluster standard errors at the 10-digit parcel (`pin10`) level. The geographic time controls vary by section. Preliminary and effective map designations are estimated separately; within each section, the preliminary-map table precedes the effective-map table.

In the tables labeled I and II, I estimates the added-to-SFHA indicator and II estimates the removed-from-SFHA indicator. The corresponding Subset columns exclude the opposite transition and the always-SFHA group (for additions) or never-SFHA group (for removals). Where shown, Symmetric uses the in-SFHA indicator, while column 0 includes addition and removal indicators together. Positive coefficients indicate higher estimated sale prices and negative coefficients indicate lower estimated prices; a log coefficient b converts to a percentage change as `100 * (exp(b) - 1)`. Stars and standard errors should be read alongside the sample and fixed-effects specification.

These tables use the existing prepared dataset `outputs/q2_marginaleffects/bldg_sfha_q2_dissertation_prepped.RDS`; despite its folder name, the models below use Q1 FEMA map-transition indicators. The 300-meter indicator comes from `data/processed/bldgswithin300mof_SFHA_arcgis.xlsx`, matched on `PIN10`. This page documents the implemented samples. It does not establish that the current tables reproduce the exact 15.9% and 18.1% estimates reported in the dissertation; that requires a separate check of the original data vintage, model, and coefficient transformation.

# Spatial spillover exclusion {#spatial-spillover-concerns}

The dissertation's second spatial check asks whether nearby comparison properties might also respond to flood-risk information. The code here retains observations outside the 300-meter list, plus properties ever added to or removed from an SFHA under either preliminary or effective maps. It therefore excludes nearby properties with no recorded transition. This is a transition-based rule, rather than a direct filter for current non-SFHA status; it can also exclude nearby always-SFHA properties. That distinction matters when comparing these results with the dissertation's description.

The first tables use property and year fixed effects on this spillover-exclusion sample. The subsequent tables add geographic time controls. Compare like-for-like transition indicators and map timing across the nearby-sample and spillover-exclusion tables; sample composition differs between the checks.

Could exclude untreated properties within the 300 m buffer around SFHA areas or I could exclude properties that were in the 1 in 500 SFHA zone but not the SFHA zone since that is essentially a bufferzone in itself.
